### Statistics about Plane Crashes
[E1] On Air France Flight 447, the pilot reported that he had slept only 1 hour before the flight had begun. This was one of the deadliest crashes in history, killing 228. The pilot's fatigue definitely played a part in the crash.
[E2] 300+ aviation accidents occurring in the U.S. that have been associated with fatigue since 1982.
[E3] Although aviation accidents are rare, when they do occur, 80% are a result of human error, and of those, 15-20% are caused by pilot fatigue.
[E4] In a survey by the British Airline Pilots' Association, 43% of the 500 participants said they had involuntarily fallen asleep in the cockpit, and of those, 31% said that they woke to find the other pilot asleep.
[E5] It can be estimated that billion(s) of USD are lost in aircraft crashes every year:
   [E5a] The average amount of money spent from 2020 to 2024 (5-year range) on major hull claims for planes was 429 million USD per year.
   [E5b] This doesn't account for other types of aircraft (helicopters, smaller aircraft, etc.). If we extrapolate and account for cargo or other materials lost in crashes, we can assume that almost another billion USD is lost per year.
[E6] Why are these a problem? Because fatigue and drowsiness, while they may not be the core part of the problem, will enhance/accelerate many potential problems by leading to bad decision making or slower reaction times.
